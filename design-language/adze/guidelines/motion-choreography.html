What moves, and why

Timing & easing covers how long a movement takes and what curve it rides. This covers the harder question: what should move at all, and what the movement is telling the person watching it. Motion in this system has exactly one job — to explain where a thing came from and where it went. Motion that decorates is motion that has to be sat through, every time, forever.

The test: remove the animation and ask whether anything became harder to follow. If nothing did, the animation was never doing work.

List to detail is one element moving, not two elements fading

A crossfade says one screen ended and another began. A shared element says that row is this page — the thumbnail the artist clicked is the thumbnail they are now looking at, so they never have to work out where they are. Reuse the element and let it transition; don't build a second copy and dissolve between them. Click either panel.

The siblings leave with --adze-ease-in at --adze-dur-fast while the target arrives with --adze-ease-out at --adze-dur. The thing you are going to is the thing that gets the time.

Entering and leaving are not the same motion

Something arriving is announcing itself: --adze-ease-out, fast start, soft landing, --adze-dur. Something leaving is getting out of the way: --adze-ease-in and a shorter duration — --adze-dur-fast. Nobody has ever wanted to wait for a dismissal. Write the leave timing as the element's resting state and let the .is-in class override it; then each direction is stated once and cannot drift.

The second one is not slower by much. It just refuses to leave, and a person who saves twenty times an hour feels every one of them.

Stagger must be capped

A per-row delay is lovely at six rows and absurd at thirty-two. Cap the index at 8 — after that every remaining row shares the last delay, so the wave still reads but the list finishes arriving. The step here is --adze-dur-fast / 4, which puts the capped total inside --adze-dur-slow. Uncapped, the last row of thirty-two lands past --adze-dur-slower, at which point the system's own rule says it stopped being a transition — and it is a very bad progress bar, because nothing is loading.

An entrance fires on enter, not on every render

A panel's entrance animation belongs to the panel appearing. Tie it to a re-render instead and it replays on every state change — which, in an admin, means it replays every time the artist saves. It does not read as an animation at that point; it reads as the screen flashing whenever you touch anything. This was a real bug here. Press each Save a few times.

Past --adze-dur-slower it is a progress state, not a transition

480ms is the ceiling. Anything longer is not a transition that needs tuning — it is a wait, and a wait needs a component that says so: a Skeleton when you know the shape of what is coming, a ProgressBar when you know how far along you are, a Spinner when you know neither. Stretching an animation to cover a slow request tells the person nothing except that the interface is fond of itself.

Under prefers-reduced-motion the choreography still holds — the order things happen in, and which element is which, survive intact. Only the travel collapses. The autoplay loops on this card stay parked in that mode; the buttons still work.
